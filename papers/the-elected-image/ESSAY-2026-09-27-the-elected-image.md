## In one page

**What he claimed.** The claim has three parts. (1) A group's ghostwheel works like the **system image**
a desktop field-support team relies on: one known setup on every machine, so the team can be trained to
answer calls about it. (2) The same logic ties C4's work together in two ways. The first is two kinds of
risk: *social* risk, which comes **in** through people, and *technical* risk, which goes **out** at a
boundary. The second is two practices: **double-entry books** and **bold guessing that leads with the
current best answer**. (3) C4 might be the **first tool in human history** made for no other reason than
to let peers elect the systems they work in.

**What the demonstration shows.**

- **Part 1 holds once one word is added.** The image has to be *elected*, chosen by the people who will
  support each other on it. With that word, the field-support team's need for control and the ordinary
  program's gift of freedom are the same rule, seen from two different places (Movements I to III). The
  tree already holds every piece of this. It defines parity as obligations kept, not identical bytes. It
  says users may cut parts out, "and that is proper". It says an outer ring never inherits an inner ring's
  rules. No file puts the pieces together.
- **Part 2 holds halfway.** The way *out* is well built: 38 files mention egress, and there is an egress
  spell. The way *in* through people is practised: the ingest spell asks consent before copying other
  people's facts, and there is a gate for imported skills. But the way in has no name. And the one real
  gap the tree found this week came in socially and got past the check at the way out. That gap is other
  people's screen names inside chat logs (Movement IV). Double entry is practised for links between
  files. It is not yet practised for the image itself. "Lead with the best current guess and keep the
  history behind it" is the house rule, but the house still writes its correction history on top
  (Movement V).
- **Part 3 does not hold as worded** (Movement VI). Several earlier works each did part of it first:
  GNU (1985), the wiki (1995), Debian's social contract and constitution (1997 and 1998), the
  Scandinavian union design projects (1970 to 1985), and Mastodon's per-server rules. No artifact can
  show "no motive other than". His own sentence also names a second goal. A narrower claim survives my
  search: **a tool that lets a group elect its own working agreements, ring by ring, keeps them checkable
  and portable, and has an AI assistant in the loop that is bound by them.** I found nothing earlier that
  does all of that. That tells us about my search. It does not tell us about history.

**What is his to decide** (Coda, and `decisions.json`): which wording of the priority claim to adopt; whether
the image picture goes into `GHOSTWHEEL.org`; whether "ingress" becomes a registered word; who reads this
first (I recommend Dee, who reached the claim with him); and whether the elected image gets a
trial balance.

## I. The field team and the standard image: why control makes sense there

His picture starts with a team that answers calls. OBSERVED, his words (prompt, same paragraph):

> "The goal of that team is a clear picture, sufficient to train a pod to answer calls about whatever
> crash or trouble a given user runs into."

> "what we can do to prep people helping end users will be specifically bounded by the freedoms end-users
> have, usually meaning ability to install and run apps and otherwise perform activities that aren't
> strictly controlled."

> "even foreseeable problems specific to the app the user selected to install will hit us cone because
> foresight requires the act of planning to realize, however foregone the conclusion."

The trade has a name for what that team relies on. OBSERVED, in the desktop-management literature: a
*Standard Operating Environment* is "a standard implementation of an operating system and its associated
software", and "the major advantage of an SOE in a business environment is the reduction in the cost and
time taken to deploy, configure, maintain, support and manage computers" (*Standard Operating
Environment*, Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Operating_Environment). A *golden image*
is "a template for a virtual machine, virtual desktop, server or hard disk drive", used to "save time and
ensure consistency" (*What is golden image?*, TechTarget,
https://www.techtarget.com/searchitoperations/definition/golden-image).

**JUDGED.** The standard image is the support team's map made true. A pod can be trained only on a state
it can know in advance. Every freedom left to the user is a part of the map the pod cannot train for. So
his sentence is exact, not a figure of speech: *how well a support team can prepare is bounded by what the
users are free to change.* Control, in that setting, is not a taste for domination. It is the cost of
being able to promise "we can help you". The team that sets the image pays for that promise by answering
the calls.

It is also clear who holds the power there. OBSERVED, the tree kept one role observation from his
SAVANNAH experience: "**Operational authority follows whoever decides when the machines reboot** — not
title" (`MEMORY.md:124`; the same, with his original words, at `decks/c4-metalanguage.deck.md:141-146`).
In a field-support shop, the people who own the image decide when machines reboot. The image *is* the
authority.

Two further observations change what "image" must mean.

- **OBSERVED, domain.** The trade has itself moved away from byte-identical images. "The introduction of
  bring-your-own-device (BYOD) … has led many organisations to reconsider the use of an SOE". Some have
  "implemented an unmanaged operating environment where users manage and maintain their own devices,
  subject to policies enforcing minimum standards" (same Wikipedia article). The newer tools say *desired
  state*, not *identical copy*: "Puppet uses a declarative language to describe the *desired state* of
  hosts". Where the agent "finds differences between the desired state … and the actual state, known as
  drifts", it corrects them and "reports correction results back" (*Introducing configuration management
  by using Puppet*, Red Hat Satellite 6.16,
  https://docs.redhat.com/en/documentation/red_hat_satellite/6.16/html/managing_configurations_by_using_puppet_integration/introducing-configuration-management-by-using-puppet).
- **OBSERVED, tree.** The tree reached the same place without being told. When he moved between machines,
  the rule was: "**Zero-parity is obligations, not file-equality.** Parity is measured by the obligations
  smoke-test: IRC/email reachable, ERC/Emacs patch loop runs from H:, card-games.el dev loop builds …,
  Dad+Bryant surfaces intact" (`h/PAPER-2026-08-18-h-move-safest-series.md:35-37`). The tree also keeps
  the literal, byte-level kind of parity where bytes matter. `PLUGIN-PARITY` counts plugin modules that
  are byte-identical between the copy being worked on and the copy shipped. It went "CLOSED (11
  identical) → OPEN (10 identical, 1 differs)" on a session's own edit, and it said so
  (`q/build-shapes-2026-09-17/ROUNDTRIP.md:182`, `:74-75`).

**JUDGED.** So the "image" that survives from his metaphor is not a disk copied to every machine. It is
**a declared list of what must keep working, checked on every device, with drift reported rather than
hidden.** Field support needs that much control. It does not need more.

*Concern [DOMAIN].* To most technical readers "system image" means a locked-down machine the user cannot
change. Put at C4's front door, the word could teach the opposite of what he means. **Falsifier:** give the
one-paragraph image picture (the "In one page" above) to a reader who has never seen C4. Ask what a member
of a ring may change on their own machine. If they answer "anything, and it gets shown", the concern
fails.

## II. The general-use program: why freedom makes sense there

His second sentence names the other setting. OBSERVED (prompt, same paragraph):

> "That control, while it's not what we think about when we are designing an application for general use
> (in which case we think about offering the users the freedom to choose among the programs capability as
> freely as makes sense when deciding how they will use the program), makes perfect sense in context of
> desktop field support teams' problems."

The movement he serves put this in writing four decades ago. OBSERVED: "The freedom to study how the program
works, and change it to make it do what you wish" is freedom 1. The first published definition (GNU's
Bulletin, February 1986) put its purpose as "the freedom to change a program, so that you can control it
instead of it controlling you" (*The Free Software Definition*, Wikipedia,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Free_Software_Definition).

The tree holds the same position, and holds it hard.

- OBSERVED: his "#1 trepidation and #1 requirement in one": the user may delete the parts of what we made
  that they do not want, *"and that is proper."* (`decks/degrade-honestly.deck.md:3-5`). The test that
  follows is "when someone deletes the part of C4 they don't like, does what remains LIE?" (`:21-22`).
- OBSERVED: "C4 treats an organization's context and a local user's context as equals, and assumes neither
  is the default. C4's own best practice does not govern what someone else does with their computer."
  (`d/door/36-his-attribution.part:14-16`).
- OBSERVED: "**elect-not-adopt (BLESSED).** C4 is a discipline you ELECT, not a dialect you must ADOPT"
  (`model.md:497`).

**JUDGED.** Freedom is the right default for a general-use program for a plain reason. The program's
author does not support the user's machine, cannot know its image, and has promised nothing about it. An
author who withholds freedom is taking control without taking on the duty that justifies control.

The two settings have already collided in the tree, and he named the collision himself. OBSERVED
(`q/2026-09-26-onward-readiness/PROMPT.md:23`):

> "as I consider things from the perspective of a user dropping GHOSTWHEEL.org into a live, complicated
> and well established iT supported shop: I see very little of deference and vast amounts of arrogance."

In the same passage he describes his small `archive-markdown.sh`. He had called it by hand "several dozen
times". Then it was rewritten into "a much more defensive piece of coding", and "I have not called it
manually a single time" since (same line; the tensions track files this as T08, REAL).

**JUDGED.** That is Movement I and Movement II meeting in the wrong order. The tools behaved as though
they *were* the image, refusing and gating, inside a shop that already had an image and a team that owns
its reboots. A program that lives in someone else's image has to defer. A program that behaves like the
image has to have been elected. The defensive rewrite was neither.

*Concern [HIS].* "Deference" can be over-read as "C4 has no floor". **Falsifier:** the machine-enforced
floor at `model.md:497-498` ("Reduce Claude-restrictions toward the machine-enforced FLOOR") is not empty.
If deference is read as removing it, the concern holds. If the floor is kept and only the rest defers,
the concern fails.

## III. The rings: who elected the image

Movements I and II are not two values in a tug-of-war. They are two answers to one question: **who
promised support, to whom, and who chose the state that support is promised over?** He gave the key the
day before, in the puppet analogy. OBSERVED (his words, 2026-09-26, carried in today's brief):

> "consider that C4 is like a tool for making a puppet (ghostwheel) that might have multiple operators. If
> there's one operator it makes perfect sense that the user will want four simple to work wires, compared
> to the hard to rewire by other means balancing and pressure mechanics. If we have a team we have more
> puppeters but the audience isn't implicitly more people than are in the room. When we think in org
> context that changes because an org is implicitly what a team isn't: consider going to our club vs
> performing at a club: the latter exists to provide a surface to people other than the organizers. That
> group needs to show how the wires are working if they aren't working according to the means described
> by the version of the C4/ghostwheel manuals users see while using the system, it's apps, etc."

The tree already has the ring rule that lets this analogy carry weight. OBSERVED: "an inner ring may
require more of its members than the outer ring requires of the public; the outer never inherits the
inner's requirement" (`analysis/ceremony/PAPER-2026-09-24-users-pick-ceremonies.md:115-117`). That paper
also quotes SAVANNAH's August charter (cited there by path, not copied): "consent is required always; its
SHAPE is ring-scoped and elected, never inherited", and "every artifact CARRIES its terms fully"
(`:46-50`).

Put the image into that frame:

| ring | who supports whom | who chose the image | control that is legitimate | what a member keeps |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **one operator** | himself | himself | any; he is both the pod and the caller | everything; "four simple to work wires" |
| **a team, lab partners** | each other, in the room | the members, together, in writing | what they elected, for as long as they keep electing it | exit, and an honest departure that shows (Movement V) |
| **an org** | a support team supports users who did not write the image | the org | the image, **if** it shows how the wires work wherever they differ from the manual | the right to see how the wires work; freedom 1 on the programs themselves |
| **a public club** | nobody promised support | nobody on behalf of the audience | none beyond the terms each artifact carries | all four freedoms |

**JUDGED.** Read this way, the field-support team and the general-use program are the org row and the
public row. His claim is that a ghostwheel lets the **team row** exist on purpose. Peers can write the
image themselves, support each other on it, and keep the right to leave. That is what "elect" adds to
"image". Control inside the ring is legitimate because the people bound by it chose it and can un-choose
it. Freedom survives at the ring's edge, where it matters.

This reconciling move is not new in governance, and the essay should say so. OBSERVED: Ostrom's principles
for long-lived commons include "Collective-choice arrangements, that allow most resource appropriators to
participate in the decision-making process", "Effective monitoring (monitors accountable to the
appropriators, or are the appropriators)", and "Nested enterprises … with small local CPRs at the base
level" (*Elinor Ostrom's Eight Commons Governance Design Principles*, P2P Foundation Wiki,
https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Elinor_Ostrom%E2%80%99s_Eight_Commons_Governance_Design_Principles). The
rings are nested enterprises. The monitors in the team row are the members themselves. **JUDGED:** what
is new is applying this to a machine's configuration and to an AI assistant's working rules, not the
shape itself.

**Where the ring model breaks.** Three places, found by pressing on it.

1. **The member who disagrees** (the U02 track). The tree says what the ring may require. It does not say
   what a member keeps when they disagree with an election and stay. Degrade-honestly gives half an
   answer: they may remove the part, and the remainder must not lie. The other half is still missing: how
   the rest of the ring *sees* that one member's machine differs. That half is bookkeeping, and it belongs
   to Movement V.
2. **The people in the logs.** The puppet analogy has operators and an audience. It has no place for the
   people the puppet *depicts*. OBSERVED, the review that applied the analogy on 2026-09-26: "The vocab
   exemption decides how *other people's* names appear. Those people are not operators. They are in the
   logs, not in the room." (`magic-number-theory/REVIEW-2026-09-26-ingest-writing-firing.md:158-159`).
   They are not a ring at all. They came in through a door, which is Movement IV.
3. **Who reboots.** In an org, the image owner decides reboots, and so holds authority (`MEMORY.md:124`).
   An elected image needs someone the ring names to decide when its machines restart, update or change
   image. Nothing in the tree names that role for a ring. *Concern [MINE]:* this is a gap in the
   analogy's use, not in his thesis. **Falsifier:** a ring's written terms (a `GHOSTWHEEL.org` build's
   Terms of Service, `GHOSTWHEEL.org:24-25`) that already say who changes the image, and when.

## IV. Ingress and egress: where the risk comes in, and where it goes out

His parenthesis pairs two words: "the social (ingress) and technical (egress) risk (mitigation) spaces".
Here is what the tree has for each.

**Egress (what leaves, at a named technical boundary) is well built.** OBSERVED:

- 38 files in the snapshot mention egress (`grep -rliE 'egress'`, excluding `.json`, `.sha256` and
  `.gpg`).
- The tree already separates the class, the act and the record: "**EGRESS** a **class** on a fact",
  "**WITHHOLD** an **act** — moving or refusing to move a specific artifact at a specific boundary",
  "**WITHHELD** the **ledger**" (`facts/withhold-is-the-act-egress-is-the-class.md:25-29`).
- An egress spell was written today from his words of 2026-09-26: "the third actor is the egress, which
  is the solution to keeping the other two N(1) in terms of lookups, checks included"
  (`spells/egress/SPELL.md:5-8`; his original at `q/2026-09-26-onward-readiness/PROMPT.md:25`).

**Ingress (what comes in through people) is practised but has no name.** OBSERVED:

- The word "ingress" appears in exactly 2 files, and both are about network code: "Strip text properties
  on ingress" (`journal/HANDOFF-2026-07-29-cg-net-fixes.md:38`; the other is
  `decks/accountability-first.deck.md:101`).
- The practice exists without the word, in at least three places. The ingest spell has a standing
  constraint: "A third party's person-facts in the material need consent before they are copied anywhere
  the source did not put them" (`spells/ingest/SPELL.md:70-71`). The garden's `candor-gate` skill, by its
  own description, clears provenance and "consent for third-party or family person-facts before
  persistence" before an imported skill acts. And `GHOSTWHEEL.org:33-37` describes a peer joining a ring
  "given a successful negotiation of governance during the peering ceremony". That third one is a door a
  person walks through, and it is not yet built.

**One case shows why both sides are needed.** OBSERVED
(`magic-number-theory/REVIEW-2026-09-26-ingest-writing-firing.md:89-99`): ingestion of chat logs leaves
certain screen names in clear. The checker counts them as noise. The publication guard "only knows
addressed names". So "a meme that later travels (a quote, a paper) could carry a person's handle past
every check." The review is careful: "Nothing has travelled … This is a forecast, not a finding."

**JUDGED.** That one case is his thesis in miniature. The risk came in *socially*: other people's names,
arriving inside his logs. The check at the way *out* can only compare against a list, and someone had to
write that list at the way *in*. **An egress check is only as good as the ingress record it checks
against.** He also warned what happens if the shop tries to solve social risk at the exit alone: the
"regular and distracting egress focused edge cases which are so ready to become demanding routines
implicitly forming expectations on users" (`q/2026-09-26-onward-readiness/PROMPT.md:27`). A record made
at the door is cheaper. It is one line, written when the thing arrives, by the person who let it in.

The mapping he asked for, both sides, both settings:

| | desktop field support | C4 / ghostwheel |
|---|---|---|
| **comes in through people (ingress)** | a self-installed app; a new hire's habits; the call itself, where the trouble arrives as one person's account of it | a lab partner's drop; a chat log with other people's names in it; an imported skill; a new peer joining a ring; a prompt |
| **how it is mitigated (socially)** | training; an acceptable-use agreement; "tell us what you installed" | consent before copying person-facts; provenance before an imported skill acts; the peering ceremony's disclosures; a line in the intake record |
| **goes out at a boundary (egress)** | the image rollout; a patch; telemetry; closing the ticket | a publish; a zip to a partner; a `git push`; the publication shelf |
| **how it is mitigated (technically)** | change control; signed packages | the egress spell; the salted list of names checked at publication; the withheld ledger |

**JUDGED, a proposal that follows the tree's own pattern.** Give ingress the same three parts egress
already has: **INGRESS** is the *class* (what came in through people, and whose facts it carries).
**INTAKE** is the *act*: the tree already says `c4 intake` (`MEMORY.md:2719`). The *ledger* is the
provenance record the ingest spell already writes (`PROVENANCE.tsv`, same line). Nothing new is built.
A name is given to what is already done, so that the egress check can ask the ingress record a question.

*Concern [MINE].* A new registered word adds to the vocabulary a newcomer must learn. **Falsifier:** if
the plain phrase "what came in through people" does the same work in the ingest spell and the egress
spell without confusion, the word is not needed, and option B of decision D3 wins.

## V. Two practices: double-entry books and resume-forward guesswork

He named two practices that make the union work "in practice". Each gets a plain definition, a reason it
belongs to the field-support picture, and a test of whether the tree does it.

### Double-entry books

**Plainly:** every movement is written down twice, once from each side, so that when the two sides are
added up, a missing side shows at once. The value is not in the second copy. The value is in the **trial
balance**, the check that compares the two sides and fails when they disagree.

**Why it belongs to the image.** A managed desktop already keeps double books without calling them that.
The image is the *declared* side. The device's report is the *actual* side. Drift is the difference, and
it gets reported (the Puppet passage in Movement I). For an elected image, the two sides are **what the
ring agreed** and **what each member's machine actually runs**. Kept that way, degrade-honestly stops
being an alarm and becomes a line in the books: *this member removed the signing hook, so authorship is
unverified here.* The ceremony paper already requires this: "The cross-check must survive the
substitution … say aloud that authorship is now unverified"
(`analysis/ceremony/PAPER-2026-09-24-users-pick-ceremonies.md:119-122`). *In passing:* his analogy says
"puppet", and the trade's best-known desired-state tool is called Puppet. The coincidence points the
right way. It proves nothing.

**Does the tree do it?** OBSERVED:

- **Yes, for links.** His words, 2026-09-17: "use your best double entry bookkeeping (doubly linked lists,
  cross-referencing both/n-ways)" (`q/junctures/README.md:12-13`). The rule: "Every link is recorded at
  both ends, by one run, and a trial balance fails when they disagree" (`:141`). And it runs:
  `BALANCED finding spec=27 meta=27 backlinks=27 in-model=27`
  (`q/universes/derived-file-disposition.md:41`).
- **Yes, for plugin copies.** PLUGIN-PARITY went OPEN on the session's own edit and said so (Movement I).
  The books worked.
- **No, for the elected image.** No list of "what this ring agreed its machines run" exists to balance
  against. The nearest thing is `GHOSTWHEEL.org`'s "What every build carries" (Terms of Service, Privacy
  Policy, persona, terms, `:18-29`). It is the start of the declared side, and there is no actual side.
- **No, in three smaller places.** The screen-name gap is an unbalanced pair: the intake leaves names in
  clear, and the exit list does not know them (Movement IV). The drift budget "genuinely mutated across
  sources" with three figures (tensions track T13, REAL). And `evidence/share-hold.tsv` carries signed
  authority with zero paths. The tree's own reading of that is exact: "It is evidence that a mechanism
  has never run" (`facts/withhold-is-the-act-egress-is-the-class.md:53-56`).

**Where double entry turns into ceremony.** The tree answers this itself: "A convention says *remember to
record it at the other end*. Conventions decay silently … Here the two entries are written by one run
from one measurement" (`q/junctures/README.md:158-161`). **JUDGED:** that is the test. If a person has to
remember to write the second side, it is ceremony, and his "don't make ceremony" applies. If one run
writes both sides from one measurement, it is bookkeeping, and it pays.

### Resume-forward guesswork

**Plainly:** when you do not know, make the best guess you can, say it is a guess, and lead with it. Keep
the story of earlier guesses and corrections *behind* it, in a separate history (a "resume"). Do not keep
it in front, where it buries the current answer.

OBSERVED, the rule is his: "be brave in guessing, brave in writing, bold in asserting our learnings
*somewhere*, boldest in leading with the best and most current information", and "We can always make
RESUME.md … if we need to establish or rehome a learning history" (`d/door/00-keeper-top-block.part:21`).
Also his: "It's a resume builder but that's literally what we are making — self-healing resume builder
that just asks you to better define terms we get wrong." (`decks/self-healing-resume.deck.md:9-11`).

**Why it belongs to field support.** His own sentence says foresight "requires the act of planning to
realize". A pod cannot plan for an app nobody told it about. It *can* guess boldly from pattern, say it is
guessing, fix the call, and write what happened where the next pod will find it. The trade keeps that
second half as a *known error* record: "A Known Error Database (KEDB), which documents the root cause, any
available workarounds, and the status of fix efforts" (*Known error*, Wikipedia,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Known_error). **JUDGED:** resume-forward guesswork is how a group supports
what it elected *and* what its members added on their own. The image covers what was planned. The guess
covers the rest. The resume turns this week's guess into next week's training.

**Does the tree do it?** OBSERVED:

- **The guessing, increasingly.** The cost of *not* guessing is measured. Of eight refusals grounded in
  "my model of the project", five were later retracted, a rate of 62%
  (`facts/refusals-sort-by-whose-fact-they-are-about.md:10`).
- **The "resume behind" half, no.** Four door parts carry their correction history inside the rule text

## VI. "The first tool in human history": the claim against prior art

**JUDGED, word by word.**

- **"first tool in human history" does not survive.** A search can find a counterexample. It cannot
  prove there is none. For the core phrase "peers elect systems designed around communication and
  collaboration preferences", Debian, the wiki and Mastodon each come close enough that a fair reader
  would dispute "first".
- **"specifically created with no motive other than" does not survive as a test.** Motive does not show in
  an artifact. And his own sentence names a second goal in the next breath: "even notwithstanding the the
  practical goal of enabling teams to build entire bespoke systems". The tree names others too: making
  "consent and accountability legible at the seams" (`README.md:3-4`), and letting users "keep each other
  honest" about quiet changes (`MEMORY.md:103-106`). They all serve the one goal. They are still other
  motives.
- **What survives my search** is a *combination*. Earlier works have the parts separately:
  1. **the thing elected is the group's working agreements**: how members communicate, collaborate and
     check each other, including ceremonies, filters and defaults. It is not only the software
     (`GHOSTWHEEL.org:31-45`, "Filters announce themselves").
  2. **electable per ring**, with the outer ring never inheriting the inner's rules (ceremony paper
     `:115-117`).
  3. **checkable**: every mechanism announces itself, and a trial balance can show where a member departs
     (Movement V; the part the tree has not yet built for the image).
  4. **portable**: "every artifact CARRIES its terms fully" (ceremony paper `:50`), and "rebuilding with
     other defaults is one sentence from the person the build is for" (`GHOSTWHEEL.org:49-50`).
  5. **an AI assistant in the loop, bound by the elected agreement**, not by the vendor's.

  AGENTS.md has 1, 4 and part of 5. Debian has 1, part of 2 and part of 3, and no AI. Mastodon has part
  of 1 and part of 4. Collective Constitutional AI has 5 without 2 or 4. I found no earlier tool with all
  five.
- **One more honest limit.** C4 is *designed* to do all five. It is not yet *doing* all five for a second
  group. The shop's own check reported `PLUGIN-INSTALLED` OPEN: "nothing at `~/.claude/plugins`"
  (`q/build-shapes-2026-09-17/ROUNDTRIP.md:183`). The claim is about what the tool was made for, and it
  should say so.

*Concern [DOMAIN].* A priority claim published in the free-software world will be read by people who
were in Debian, on wikis, and in the union projects. **Falsifier:** show the recommended wording (Coda,
option B) to one such reader. If they name a prior tool with all five parts, the recommendation is wrong
and the claim should shrink again.

## The losers kept on the table

**L1. The image is the wrong picture. (Stated at its strongest.)** A system image is the thing an
organization imposes on people who did not choose it. It is the symbol of the managed desktop he warned
against when he saw "very little of deference and vast amounts of arrogance" in an IT-supported shop.
Borrowing the picture teaches C4 to think like the image owner. Once "image" is at the front door, every
later design will drift toward uniformity, because that is what the word means to everyone who has
worked a help desk. The one operator and the public club gain nothing from the picture. Only the team
row does, and even there "agreement" says it better than "image". **Why it loses here:** the trade itself
has moved from identical disks to declared obligations and reported drift. The tree defines parity the
same way. And the picture explains, in words a field technician already owns, why a group might *want*
limits: so it can help each other. No other plain picture in the tree does that. **Where it could still
win:** if the stranger test in Movement I fails. Then the picture belongs inside `GHOSTWHEEL.org` for
people who already know C4, and not at any front door.

**L2. Double entry is ceremony.** He has said "don't make ceremony" many times. He called the BANK
"basically underpowered spreadsheets" (`q/2026-09-26-onward-readiness/PROMPT.md:23`). Every second entry
is work someone did instead of the job. **Why it loses:** only where one run writes both sides from one
measurement, as the juncture rule requires. **Where it wins:** everywhere a person must remember the
second side. There, the loser is right, and the book should not be kept.

**L3. Keep his sentence exactly as he said it. Boldness is the practice.** Resume-forward guesswork says:
lead with the boldest current guess and let correction come. "First in human history" is a bold guess,
and publishing it invites exactly the correction that makes a self-healing resume heal. **Why it loses
for publication:** the only evidence available, a search, cannot correct it. A miss proves nothing, so
the guess can never be tested and never heal. "No motive other than" is also contradicted inside the
same sentence. **Where it wins:** in conversation, with Dee, as the hypothesis this essay tested. That is
where it came from, and where it did its work.
