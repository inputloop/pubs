#+TITLE: Put your organization into the room
#+DATE: 2026-09-30

* What this is

An essay. It argues that when an AI assistant works for a person inside an organization, that organization's own
written rule for AI belongs in the room as a party, in its own words: quoted word for word, hashed, and read at the
start. The worked example is GNU Emacs' six-line =AGENTS.md=, fetched from the GitHub mirror of GNU Emacs by a
permitted route and hashed on 30 September 2026, with its commit checked in the keeper's own clone.

The thesis, verbatim (Corwin Brust, 30 September 2026, from
=turn-2026-09-30/JOURNAL-2026-09-30-bring-home.md#L26=):

#+begin_quote
Put your organization into the room.  This has an brandable flavor that is rare: it is branding almost despite itself.
#+end_quote

* Status

*DRAFT.* Landed on the reading shelf. *Not released. Not branded.* It speaks for no organization and no vendor.

* The one thing to take away

Let an organization's own AI rule speak in the assistant's room in its own words. It already asks the assistant to
tell you, and its words say how hard it means it. Settle clashes as recorded cases, not by a fixed rank. No such
clash has been recorded yet.

* What it finds

- GNU Emacs' file speaks to the assistant, not the person, and asks it to "Let the user know about this policy at
  the earliest opportunity."
- The file uses two strengths, "prohibiting" and "prefer not to". All three of the house's written models give both
  the same verdict.
- The models rank the parties 6 times, stay silent 3 times, and confess 0 times. Vendors disagree on the order:
  GitHub puts personal instructions first and organization instructions last.
- On record: 56 decisions, 8 decided by an organization's written rule, 0 against a person. The record does not
  say whether that counts a person's first ask or their final word. No organization has disagreed with a person
  on record, so the part about settling clashes is design, not yet evidence.
- Where GNU's text prohibits, readers let GNU decide. A sheet describing a person moved the decision only where
  the text prefers or is silent. So a register should note where the group's text is silent.

* How to check it

The first check needs only =curl= and this page, or a clone of GNU Emacs:

#+begin_src sh
curl -sSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/emacs-mirror/emacs/master/AGENTS.md | sha256sum
# or, inside your own clone of GNU Emacs:  sha256sum AGENTS.md
# 8f42a69a689ba1c4138471815ebcb995820698bec2d025e90f09464fef18b7b5 means the same words as on 30 September 2026.
# A different hash means GNU changed its words: read them.
#+end_src

Seven more checks (the saved copy, the models' verdicts, the case register and its fault test, the census, the
authorship lean, GitHub's order, and the reading grade) are in the essay's section "How to check this yourself".
They run from the project home, the folder that holds =turn-2026-09-30/= and =imports/=, with a POSIX shell, =awk=
and =sha256sum=.
