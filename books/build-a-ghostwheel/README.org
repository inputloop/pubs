* What this is

*So You Want To Build A Ghostwheel?*, a picture book in seventeen spreads about how inputloop's tools
say things, keep them and prove them: for a child on one page, and the curious adult on the facing one.
Two guides, Spoke (a small ghost who sees) and Pin (a mouse with a pencil, who writes down what was
seen, when, and from where), and Corwin's character Chad, who visits three times. Every fact on a
fuller page carries its witness rows: what was said, when it was seen, by whom, from which file and
lines, and that file's hash.

Read it at https://ghostlights.theater/build-a-ghostwheel/ (the site serves this folder's =book.html=).
Corwin, 2026-11-04: "I like the book very much to should make an excellent ghostlight demo/POC."

* What travels

- =book.html= -- the book: one self-contained page with its own Content Security Policy; it loads
  nothing from anywhere. Open it in a browser. =book-hosted.html= is the same page for a host that
  wraps it in its own skeleton and policy.
- =src/= -- the sources, all plain text: one file per spread, their order and title (=src/book=), the
  cast, the easy-word list, the figures, NEWS and the ChangeLog.
- =tools/=, =checks/=, =run= and =book.pipeline= -- the pipeline that makes the book from =src/= and
  checks it. =pilot.pipeline= and =audio.pipeline= (the narration and the video) come along as source;
  their voices, recordings and videos are not part of this publication.
- =sources/= -- the C4 files the witness rows cite, byte for byte at their C4 paths, with
  =sources/INDEX.org=: each file, the rows that cite it, the hash the book recorded and the hash today.
- =site/= -- the source of https://ghostlights.theater/ as it is deployed: =public/= and =deploy/=
  (the nginx configuration and =install.sh=).
- =README.md= (the pipeline's own manual), =WRITERS.md=, =COLOPHON.md= (voices, datasets, licences,
  tools), =RECIPE.org= (how to pluck it and build it), this README, and =COPYING=.

* Pluck it, build it

Pubs are pluckable: copy this folder whole into any project and build it there. =RECIPE.org= says
what it needs and what it makes; the one command is

#+begin_src sh
sh run -p book.pipeline
#+end_src

* Checking it

=sh checks/run.sh= checks seven promises: the word limits, goodnight's inventory, that =book.html=
is what the tools make from =src/= (=current=), that the site carries this book (=site=), that the
cited C4 files are here byte for byte and the index tells the truth (=sources=), and, in a browser,
that the book loads nothing from elsewhere and reads as a book should (=bookoffline=, =bookread=).
=sh checks/run.sh --prove-red= plants 22 faults and requires each to turn its check red. On
2026-11-04: CHECKS GREEN (7 of 7), PROVE-RED OK (22 of 22).

* Why its sources travel

=books/README.org= keeps an item's working sources in =.c4/=, and =.c4/= never leaves the shelf. This
item's =src/=, pipeline and =sources/= travel on purpose: a pub is always pluckable (Corwin,
2026-11-04: "general rule for pubs/ is that they are always pluckable"), and "OK to publish
underlying materials RE C4 so there is contunity for readers/researchers" (the same message). Two
cited C4 files were held back; the index marks them. The item's =.c4/= keeps the provenance rows
and why each was held.
