* What this is

The front-door instructions for =<H>:/pub/=. The =.seed= says what the surface *is*; this
file says what you *do* — lift material in (PACK), cut something a stranger can hold
(UNPACK/RELEASE), keep copies current (JANITORIAL), and check before anything travels that
it does not name your machine (LEAK CHECK).

A note on spelling: the surface is written =<H>:/pub/=, the =.seed='s own notation, and
example paths elide the drive letter the same way (=<drive>:\lab\notes.org=). A real drive
letter is what the check hunts for, so the recipe does not write one.

* The surface, in his words

From =.seed=: =/= (the pub root) is the "finished publications stage, instructions"; each
=<item>/= sub-folder is "for publication stage, may be a work surface". Working materials are
"protected via dot folder, gate/tunnel or otherwise safe for general use/reuse" — in practice
the =.c4/= dot-folder inside each item. An item not ready for branding "may so indicate with a
'tombstone' (zero byte) COPYING or by creating that file with whatever informational context
is needed to scope these contexts".

And the rule this whole recipe serves, his: do not leak "details of my systems
unnecessarily with these materials in publication apparatus (nor content proper, obviously,
unless such detail happens to form some material record and so we shrug at leaving whatever
path showing for that reason)".

*Unnecessarily* is the load-bearing word. No tool can tell a leak from a record. The tool
points; you decide.

* KINDS — one folder per kind of publication (2026-09-25)

His word, 2026-09-25: finished publications "can be organized by kind such as to give a
=/h/pub/books/NAME= scheme, enabling roll-up pub archives by publication type". So the root holds
one folder per KIND, and each kind holds its items; everything the =.seed= says about an =<item>/=
now holds one level down, at =<kind>/<item>/=:

| kind       | what goes there                                     | items (2026-09-25)       |
|------------+-----------------------------------------------------+--------------------------|
| =books/=   | book-shaped work: a book, an omnibus, a game book   | the four game books      |
| =papers/=  | a research paper or thesis walked up to by a comrade | =motive-inversion=, =users-pick-ceremonies= |
| =sites/=   | a web site's publishable tree                       | =beyondconventions.com=  |
| =persona/= | persona sheets, which ship separately (see PERSONA)  | twelve sheets            |
| =posts/=   | a series of short pieces for a blog (=bin/pb2pk=)    | =graders-tales=          |
| =canon/=   | the keeper's signed values text, seal carried with it | =principles= (2026-09-29) |

A new kind is a new folder here and a new row above; nothing else changes. A roll-up archive
of one kind is =<kind>/= packed whole, and each item in it still unpacks alone (the
self-contained rule below). Book-shaped work in progress lives in the lab's =projects/books/=
until it is finished; the ON turn will re-home the lab side under =p/<SLUG>=, not this surface.

* WRITING — the writing programme lands here as it is written (2026-09-27)

His word, 2026-09-27: "/h/pub is not, itself, the internet. Doing this for each piece as soon as
possible is a good way to be very sure the writing actually landed somewhere I can read it locally."

So a piece of writing is not finished when it exists in the lab. It is finished when it is on this
shelf, checked, where he reads. The rule:

1. *Land each piece in the same sitting it is written.* Not in a batch at the end, not after review:
   the shelf is a reading room, not a release. A later edit is a re-land (PACK step 5), and the
   changed hash is the story.
2. *One item per subject*, kind =papers/= unless the piece is book-shaped or a post. The lab keeps
   the working copy (=c4/writing/<subject>/=, or the subject's own folder such as
   =c4/magic-number-theory/=); the item holds the lifted copy and its PROVENANCE row.
3. *What travels*: the work itself (the dated file whose prefix names its form: ESSAY, PAPER,
   DISS, LENS, RAD, COMPARE, DESIGN, CARD, EXPERIMENT, REVIEW), any erratum NOTE filed beside it,
   and any check a stranger can re-run (a =.sh=, =.awk=, =.py= the work names as its proof).
4. *What is lifted but does not travel* (=--source=, into =.c4/sources/=): the decision and receipt
   files (=decisions.json=, =done.json=), the readers' tracks, blind-reader arms, drafts that a
   later edition replaced, and the brief given to the writers.
5. *What never lands*:
   - anything a work marks as for Corwin only (e.g. a REPASS written to him);
   - =Z/= material: other people's comments on his drafts, chat-log counts, private maps;
   - other people's words that they have not cleared for publication; a work may act on what a
     reader asked, but the reader's text stays where it was kept;
   - his answer surfaces: the execute-brief page and its worksheet, which are tools, not writing.
6. *Status on landing*: a tombstone =COPYING= and no branding. His 2026-09-25 bless covered "the
   writing projects so far"; a new piece is branded when he blesses it (see BRANDING).
7. *The check is the landing's receipt.* =pb1ck= runs inside =pb2pk=; its verdict goes into the
   Papers section of =.c4/TODO.org= beside the item, NOT CLEAN included, with what fired. A NOT
   CLEAN item stays landed (he reads it here) and is simply not released until it reads CLEAN.

* PACK — lifting formed material into an item

Formed material is anything already made in the lab that the publication needs: an org or
markdown note, a fact, a deck, a runnable script, a tool.

The tool for any kind but books (=pb2ld=) and persona (=pb2ps=) is =c4/bin/pb2pk=:

#+begin_src sh
c4/bin/pb2pk --kind papers --slug NAME --about "one or two sentences" --scrub --brand CC-BY-SA-4.0 \
             PAPER.md [MORE.md ...] --source WORKING-NOTE.md ...
#+end_src

1. *Copy it in, do not reference it.* The lifted copy lands in =<item>/.c4/sources/=, byte for
   byte. The standing rule, from =motive-inversion/README.org=: "*Self-contained is the rule.*
   … no 'also attach the lab', no 'the file is over in c4'".
2. *Give it a PROVENANCE row.* One line in =<item>/.c4/PROVENANCE.tsv=, tab separated:
   =local_copy=, =lab_origin=, =sha256_16_at_lift=, =lifted=, =note=. Get the hash with
   =sha256sum <file> | cut -c1-16=. The header of that file states why duplication is fine:
   "Duplication is fine because this file tells its story (Corwin's rule); a silent clone is
   the only sin."
3. *=lab_origin= is TREE-relative, always.* Write =c4/h/LENS-….md= or =c4/facts/…=. Never a
   drive letter, home directory, mount point, hostname or user name. The tree path says where
   a thing lives in the work; the absolute path says where your machine keeps it, and only one
   of those is anyone else's business. This file does not travel — but its contents get quoted
   into READMEs, and then they do.
4. *A file authored in the item takes =-= for the hash* and =(authored in this item)= for the
   origin — there was no lift, so there is nothing to compare against.
5. *Re-lift by re-hashing.* Per the PROVENANCE header: "On a re-lift, re-sha the lab origin;
   a changed sha is the observable tear, not a surprise." Update the row; do not quietly
   overwrite the copy and leave the old hash standing.

** What counts as a material record

Sometimes the path *is* the story: a pinned fact's location, a quoted session transcript, an
archived receipt that means nothing without where it sat. His rule from =.seed=: "if
instruments tell the story of the duplicates/mutated clones, then that is story and we keep
it around -at least in journaled out or archived form- in case we want to tell it later."

So a path stays when removing it would destroy the record, and goes when it is only
scaffolding. Two live examples in =motive-inversion/README.org=: the surface-model pointer at
line 48 is scaffolding (it could say "the pub seed"); the model pin at line 84 is arguably
record — it names where the model was fixed. The check reports both and touches neither.
Write the decision into the item's =.c4/= notes so the next release does not re-argue it.

* UNPACK / RELEASE — cutting a distributable

A release is what a comrade holds. Per =.seed=, the pub root carries "the set of latest
distributable paired archive/sig (given I do signing) unless for I share as text with
integrial sig, and then whatever packaging instructions and support mostly perhaps within
README.org".

1. *Run the leak check first.* =bin/pb1ck <item>= — before the archive, before the signature.
   A signature over a leak is a signed leak.
2. *=README.org= is the front door.* Built to be walked up to: what this is, the thesis or
   product in one screen, status, and enough context that a reader need not hold the shop
   vocabulary.
3. *=COPYING= carries branding or a tombstone.* Either the real license and a dated copyright
   line, or the informational tombstone scoping the pre-branding state. Branding is his
   signing motion; key at https://savannah.gnu.org/u/carlc.
4. *=.c4/= never travels* — that is what the dot-folder is for. The exception is explicit: the
   item names travelling paths, one per line, in =.c4/travels=. Nothing else under =.c4/=
   leaves, and the check enforces the same reading.
5. *Cut the pair at the pub root*: the archive and its detached signature, side by side, named
   for the item and the date. Text shared inline gets an integral (clearsigned) signature
   instead.
6. *Where a member might be held back*, prepare the share with =bin/sc3dm= first: it proves per
   member, so dropping one does not break the proof on the rest. =sc3dm= asks whether a member
   may travel; =pb1ck= asks whether what travels says too much.

* BOOKS — landing a finished book (=bin/pb2ld=)

A book is an item like any other; =pb2ld= does PACK and the LEAK CHECK for one, in order, so a
finished book lands the same way every time, alone or as a shelf.

#+begin_src sh
bin/pb2ld --dry BOOK-2-six-sides-and-a-pencil.md      # what it would do; writes nothing
bin/pb2ld BOOK-2-six-sides-and-a-pencil.md            # land one
bin/pb2ld --all AS4CE0001/book/games                  # land (or re-land) the whole shelf
#+end_src

1. *It checks before anything moves.* =gb1md --lint= must read FAULT 0 -- every odds claim the
   book makes, checked against a full dice enumeration. A FAULT refuses that book and the rest of
   a batch still lands. The plain text must be exactly what =gb1md --txt --ascii= renders from the
   Markdown; if the shelf's copy is not, a fresh render lands and the report says so.
2. *The book travels*: =<slug>.md= and =<slug>.txt= at the item root (=<slug>= is the file name
   less its =BOOK-N-= prefix).
3. *The working set is lifted* into =.c4/sources/=: the author's sheet and its =.tsv=, the
   editor's sheet, the set's design note. A real reader's grades are not lifted; the item credits
   them (see CREDITS AND HOLDS).
4. *PROVENANCE, one row per file*, origin tree-relative, never an absolute path (anything outside
   the known trees is named, not located). A re-landing re-hashes: unchanged files are left alone,
   a changed one is re-lifted and its row keeps the old hash -- the observable tear.
5. *README.org and COPYING are written only when absent.* The front door is the keeper's to edit
   and is never overwritten; COPYING stays a tombstone until he brands it.
6. *=.c4/leakwords= comes from the root's* (=<H>:/pub/.c4/leakwords=, one string per line: host
   names, and a user name only as a PATH -- a bare user name that is part of the keeper's own name
   fires on every byline). Then =pb1ck= runs; its verdict is the book's.

Exit 0 when every book landed CLEAN. Tests: =c4/tests/pb2ld-tests.sh= (dry run, batch, re-land,
the tear, a refused FAULT, an edited README, a withheld leakword; =--prove-red=).

* PERSONA — the persona shelf (=bin/pb2ps=, 2026-09-25)

His word, 2026-09-25: "PERSONA for books is the same as for software: persona ships separately.
Let's think of the PERSONA as a pub shelf, and that pub shelf as the proper persona publications
packaging surface" (and Drive's =ComputerPeople/persona/= remains a complete offsite store, which is
a different job). One item per sheet, at =persona/<name>/=:

#+begin_src sh
c4/bin/pb2ps --scrub --brand SHEET.md [SHEET.md ...]   # land, check, then brand if CLEAN
#+end_src

- What counts as a sheet is the persona server's rule, not the file name: an =actor:= line, a
  =CHARACTER SHEET= / =ITEM SHEET= / =PERSONA= heading, or front matter naming a persona model.
  Anything else is refused, and the refusal is printed. =*SECRET*= files are never opened.
- *A model of a real person* lands only with a line in =persona/.c4/consent= (name, date, how the
  word came, the version, the words themselves). This is the shelf's own rule for its own action
  (see CREDITS AND HOLDS). Without that line it is refused by name.
- =--scrub= writes machine paths in placeholder form (=<F>:\...=, =<home>=, =<mnt>/=) in the
  travelling copy /only/, and says so in the item's README and PROVENANCE. Names are never rewritten.
  A sheet that names this machine stays NOT CLEAN until its keeper changes it.
- =--brand= brands an item only once it reads CLEAN (=pb2br --license CC-BY-SA-4.0=, writing; as of
  2026-10-10 pb2br knows only the TA model's ID, so it refuses this one until it learns it).
- The shelf also offers the build's =TERMS-OF-SERVICE.md= and =PRIVACY.md= (minted by the plugin's
  =bin/tp2mt= at =make dist=) and the contributing personality, =corwin/=.
- Tests: =c4/tests/pb2ps-tests.sh= (and =AS4CE0001/tests/tp2mt-tests.sh= for the minted pair).

* CREDITS AND HOLDS — what the shelf judges, and what it does not (2026-09-25)

Corwin, 2026-09-25, correcting a first version of this section that modelled who wrote what and
whose word each item needed: "Only attribution as desired for given work products can be correct
... It is not our place to model consent outside the context of a system we are defining or
describing, and similarly we must not seek to model ownership (for copyright or any other purpose)
beyond specific bounds we can state as they become pertinent to actions they fence or otherwise
guide/check." (The first version is kept at =.c4/RECIPE-2026-09-25-holds-v1.org=.)

*Credits are as desired, and that is the only test.* The roles given so far are boundary conditions
for judging whether a credit on this shelf is correct. They don't replace the words an item already
uses:
- Corwin Brust: the principal author of note.
- Bryant: the next most principal contributing author.
- K Smith: credited as editor. (Bridgit is the pseudonym, not embargoed. The given name is
  embargoed in =.c4/leakwords=.)

An item's own byline travels as written. The shelf doesn't infer authorship from content, drafts or
history. A credit someone supplies is taken as given. It is changed only when the person it concerns
establishes it as an error, and only where that is something the shelf can act on (a README line, a
CREDITS file).

*The shelf models no ownership and no consent of its own.* It acts only on bounds that are stated
and that fence an action it takes. Each bound names its source:
- *Paths* never travel: =pb1ck='s patterns. Machine names may travel (=.c4/hosts=). Listed words may
  not (=.c4/leakwords=). A name moves between the two lists only at the keeper's word.
- *A persona model of a real person* lands on =persona/= only with a line in =persona/.c4/consent=.
  That is the persona shelf's own rule for its own action. Each line records how the word came to us
  (=said-here=, =relayed=, =signed=) and the version it was given for (=sha16=). The gauge says when
  the version has since moved. The rule asks for a recorded word, not for proof of who owns the model.
- *Third-party facts* (case facts, family, facts about named people) are fenced by the keeper's
  standing grant for what leaves the lab. An item that states them waits for his word.
- *An item's own stated terms* bind it, but only terms its author set. A status label that a tool or
  the instrument wrote into it ("DRAFT", "unblessed", "provisional") is not the author's term. The
  word *draft* has no settled meaning in this environment yet: draft among people and draft between
  one person and a system is an open seam, and these publications don't try to close it (Corwin,
  2026-09-25). The nine-frameworks paper was held on such a label. On his word the label came off
  it and three other items, and none of the tools writes it any more (=c4cs=, polygram's synth).

*A hold names the bound that fences it, and what would release it.* It is never a guess about
whose permission an item "really" needs. Where no stated bound fences an item, it isn't held.
Feedback on fit (grades, readers' reactions) is welcome at any time and never gates anything.

* BRANDING — licensing an item (=bin/pb2br=, =bin/tm1ck=)

An item lands with a TOMBSTONE =COPYING= and is branded when its keeper blesses it: "The writing
projects so far: bless. License them per our defaults" (2026-09-25).

#+begin_src sh
c4/bin/pb2br --license CC-BY-SA-4.0 <kind>/<item>       # writing (pb2br must learn this ID first)
c4/bin/pb2br --license CC-BY-4.0 <kind>/<item>          # the TA model only
c4/bin/pb2br --license GPL-3.0-or-later <kind>/<item>   # software
c4/bin/pb2br --license LGPL-3.0-or-later <kind>/<item>  # parts others link or copy in
c4/bin/tm1ck                                            # the TERMS block, word for word, in every carrier
#+end_src

A real license is never replaced by the tool. The tombstone is kept at =.c4/COPYING-tombstone-<date>=.
The README's "Pre-branding" line becomes the license line. The TERMS block below is the whole
statement; =tm1ck --carry FILE= puts it into a new carrier and =c4/man/licensing-carriers= lists them all.

* TODO VIEW — what to do next, per kind (=.c4/TODO.org=, =bin/pb1tv=)

The surface keeps one TODO, =.c4/TODO.org= (it never travels). Each item is a level-2 org heading
with a TODO keyword, a priority (=[#A]= next, =[#B]= soon, =[#C]= when there is room) and ONE
tag: its CATEGORY, one per kind (=books= =papers= =posts= =sites= =software= =cast= =terms= =spells=) plus
=recipe= for this recipe and its tools. Under the heading, the line that says what the item rests
on (=EVIDENCE:=), or what blocks it.

#+begin_src sh
bin/pb1tv .c4/TODO.org > .c4/TODO-view.html   # the top open item of every category, one page
bin/pb1tv --text .c4/TODO.org                 # the same selection as a table
#+end_src

The top item of a category is the first open one by state (NEXT, then TODO, then WAIT), then by
priority, then by place in the file -- an actionable item outranks a blocked one. Colour carries
priority only, one validated blue light to dark, and every priority is also written as its
letter. Tests: =c4/tests/pb1tv-tests.sh=.

* JANITORIAL — a currency-pull, not a daemon

The =.seed= gives automation the janitorial side: "C4 or other automation can have the
janatorial side helping to sync common use tools out for these working-group and/or staging
area stages." The shape of that sync is fixed by the PROVENANCE header, quoted whole:

#+begin_quote
SYNC MODEL (currency-pull, not a daemon): at the moment of inception of a mutation, pull a
cloak of currency over the file (bring it current); that pull IS the only local lock.
Reads always win the read-first battles. Write-races are governed by PRIORITY / value-
weighting (C4's, or the personal lens "until on"), by Corwin's deliberation — never a mutex.
There is no fixed source folder: currency is whatever pulled it last at a mutation; drift
stays legible here so a race is a story, never a silent loss.
#+end_quote

Practically: nothing runs on a timer here. Right before you change a lifted copy, pull it
current and re-hash its row. Between mutations, drift may exist and must stay legible.

* LEAK CHECK — what is looked for, and the one line to run

#+begin_src sh
bin/pb1ck <item>
#+end_src

Exit 0 clean, 1 for any leak hit or CHANGED row, 2 for usage. It removes nothing and refuses
nothing; it prints file, line, the pattern that fired, and the matched text with *nothing
either side of it*.

Eleven patterns, over every file that would travel (everything outside =.c4/=, plus whatever
=.c4/travels= names):

- *drive-letter* and *unc-path* — a Windows path or a share name.
- *session-path*, *home-path*, *home-var*, *mount-path* — =/sessions/=, =/home/<user>= or
  =/Users/<user>=, =$HOME= and its Windows spellings, =/mnt/=.
- *msys-path* — a drive path in its MSYS2 spelling, =/f/claude/...= (added 2026-09-25, when it was
  found in three landed papers and four sheets that the drive-letter pattern had let through).
- *ip-v4* and *ip-v6ish* — an address that names a box on a network.
- *email-undeclared* — any address that is not on the README's =#+AUTHOR:= or =#+EMAIL:=
  line, or listed in =.c4/mail-ok=.
- *leakword-N* — line N of =.c4/leakwords=, one string per line: your host names, user names,
  machine names, internal project names. Nothing of yours is written into the tool; it knows
  only what the item tells it. A hit reports the line number and *withholds the word*, because
  a report that echoes it has re-leaked it. Names that may appear in credit are listed per item in
  =.c4/credit=, and a hit on one reads CREDIT: shown, never dropped.
- *HOST* (never a leak) — =.c4/hosts=, read like leakwords from the item up to this root. These are
  the keeper's machines, which may be named (his word, 2026-09-25: "it is not a problem to name my
  machines"; paths are what we avoid). Each hit is shown with the name, so where a machine is named
  stays visible. Someone else's machine is theirs to name, so it stays in =.c4/leakwords=.

It also re-hashes every PROVENANCE row whose =local_copy= exists (MATCH, CHANGED — the
observable tear — MISSING, or NOHASH), warns when a =lab_origin= is not tree-relative, and
counts its findings twice, with grep and then with awk over the emitted report, printing
AGREE or DISAGREE: an uncounted tally is an unchecked one.

The report echoes matches, so the report is local. Do not put it in the item.

* How this recipe can fail (falsifiers)

- *A release goes out with a path that was nobody's business.* Check: run =pb1ck= on the
  released item; any hit you cannot defend as record is this recipe failing.
- *The check passes and the item still leaks.* Check: grep a cut archive for your own hostname
  and user name. A pass with no =.c4/leakwords= file is the likeliest cause — the report prints
  ABSENT in that case, and that line is the warning.
- *A reader cannot re-derive a lifted copy.* Check: take any PROVENANCE row and run
  =sha256sum <local_copy> | cut -c1-16=. If it does not reproduce the recorded value and no
  re-lift was recorded, the instrument stopped telling the story.
- *It fires where it should not.* This is for the pub surface only — lab work and private notes
  are out of scope.
- *A book lands whose odds are wrong.* Check: =gb1md --lint= on the landed =<slug>.md=; any FAULT
  means =pb2ld= let one through.
- *A landing overwrote the keeper's words.* Check: a README.org or COPYING whose bytes changed
  across a re-landing he did not ask for.
- *The TODO view shows the wrong top item.* Check: =pb1tv --text= against the rule above, by hand,
  for one category.
- *A person-model landed without a consent line.* Check: every =persona/<name>/= whose README
  says "person-model" has a line for =<name>= in =persona/.c4/consent=.
- *A hold with no stated bound behind it.* Check: every held TODO item names the bound that fences
  it (a pattern, a list, the consent rule, the grant, the item's own terms). A hold that names only a
  person's supposed ownership is this recipe failing.
- *A credit changed on inference.* Check: every change to a credit line cites the person who
  established the error.
- *A branded item leaks.* Check: =pb1ck= on every item whose COPYING is not a tombstone; any NOT
  CLEAN means =--brand= ran before the check.
- *A signed file on the shelf no longer verifies.* Check: =gpg --verify X.asc X= for each =.asc= under
  =canon/=. A BAD result means some tool or hand changed bytes this recipe says nothing may change.
- *A piece of writing was reported written but never landed.* Check: every work named in a hand-back
  since 2026-09-27 has an item under =papers/= (or a stated hold in the WRITING section) whose
  PROVENANCE row re-hashes MATCH against the lab copy.
- *Off-switch.* Say "no leak check on this item", or delete =bin/pb1ck=. Nothing here blocks,
  and nothing else is wired to it.
